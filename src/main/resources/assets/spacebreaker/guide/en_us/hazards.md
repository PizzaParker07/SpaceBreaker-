Three things can kill you in space: **vacuum**, **temperature** and **radiation**.

## Vacuum
Where there is no air you can hold your breath only for a few seconds. Then you start to suffocate. The answer: a four-piece suit and a full tank.

## Temperature
Your body temperature changes **slowly**; a few seconds of sun or shade will not kill you, but if you stay too long:
- In great heat you **overheat** (lunar noon: +120 °C).
- In great cold you **freeze** (lunar night: −150 °C, permanently shadowed craters even colder).

Your suit balances a range (Mk1: −190 °C to 130 °C). Even outside that range you warm up or cool down gradually at first.

> When the cold is beyond your suit's rating, the HUD shows the temperature icon and warns **BEYOND SUIT RATING** (**COLD TRAP: BEYOND SUIT RATING** in a cold trap). With the **visorRim** setting on, rime also creeps in from the visor rim; it thaws once you are back in range or inside a pressurised module. The **cold traps** in polar craters exist only on airless worlds where ice stays stable: the Moon (≈ −233 °C), Mercury (≈ −213 °C) and Ceres. Mars and Pluto have none. Go down in a heated suit or with enough thermal modules.

> **Underground is shelter:** on airless worlds, at least **4 blocks** below the surface and out of sight of the sky, the temperature stays at the day-night average (about −26 °C on the Moon). That makes lava tubes natural shelters. Cold traps are the exception.

## Micrometeorite showers
On airless planet surfaces a micrometeorite shower falls now and then (about once an hour, 40 seconds long). The action bar warns you a minute ahead: "get under a roof". In the open the grains cost health and suit wear: at most **three hearts** per shower, half that in a sealed suit. **Any roof** keeps you safe: any block overhead that stops movement (a full block, glass, a slab, a fence…) stops micrometeorites. No special shield block is needed.

## Pressure
Above **10 bar** (Venus's surface is 92 bar) a sealed suit loses 1 durability per piece every 10 seconds, but never below **75%**: it never leaks, it only wears. The HUD warns you: "92 BAR: SUIT UNDER STRAIN".

## Radiation
Outside an atmosphere and a magnetic field, radiation dose builds up. Watch it on the helmet display. The dose slowly goes down over time; take breaks on long Moon missions.

## Gravity
Every world has its real gravity. On the Moon (0.17 g) you jump far and fall slowly; fall damage is low too. Mars is 0.38 g.

> [!TIP]
> The helmet display shows all of these. If a warning lights up, head back to the base or the rocket.
