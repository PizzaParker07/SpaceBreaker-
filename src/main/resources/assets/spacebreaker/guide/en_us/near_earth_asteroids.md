The first small targets after the Moon: four tiny, airless asteroids. Bennu, for example, takes only about **4.1 km/s** of Δv from low Earth orbit. → [Asteroids](sci_asteroids)

>! None of the four has natural water ice. On Bennu and Ryugu you can get water **out of the regolith**; Eros and Itokawa have none. Bring fuel for the way back, or set up production. → [Asteroid mining](#mining)

## Bennu
{items spacebreaker:bennu_regolith spacebreaker:bennu_boulder}
A coal-dark rubble pile half a kilometre across, strewn with huge boulders, with a low bulge round its equator.

## Ryugu
{items spacebreaker:ryugu_regolith spacebreaker:ryugu_rock}
A dark, carbon-rich spinning top with a sharp ridge round its equator.

{page}
## Eros
{items spacebreaker:eros_regolith spacebreaker:eros_chondrite}
A long, stony asteroid about 17 km across, heavily cratered, with the great **Himeros** saddle on one side.

## Itokawa
{items spacebreaker:itokawa_gravel spacebreaker:itokawa_rock}
A rough, bouldery peanut of two lumps; its neck holds the smooth gravel plain of the **Muses Sea**, right where you land.

{page}
## Asteroid mining {#mining}
- **Carbonaceous asteroids (Bennu, Ryugu):** in the regolith heater the hydrated clay in the rock gives **200 mB of water** and **50 mB of CO₂**, plus coal dust 30% of the time. Split the water into oxygen and hydrogen by electrolysis: fuel on the spot. The {item spacebreaker:carbonaceous_chondrite} of Phobos and Deimos works the same way.
{recipe spacebreaker:regolith_processing/carbonaceous_water}
- **Stony asteroids (Eros, Itokawa):** the crusher gives **iron dust** and, 25% of the time, **nickel dust**. They give oxygen in the regolith heater and sinter into building blocks in the sintering furnace. → [In-Situ Resources](isru)
